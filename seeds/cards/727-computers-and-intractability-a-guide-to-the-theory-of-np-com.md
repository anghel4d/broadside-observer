# Computers and Intractability: A Guide to the Theory of NP-Completeness

## One-sentence takeaway

Garey & Johnson is the field guide to NP-completeness: reduction patterns, a huge problem catalogue, and the famous open-problem appendix.

## Why it matters here

Still the first book to pull when a Broadside or engine combinatorial bottleneck looks hard. Classify the problem against the catalogue, then decide whether to SAT-encode, approximate, or restrict — don’t reinvent Karp’s list.

## Key ideas

- Standard reduction patterns (restriction, local replacement, component design) are taught as a craft.
- The appendix catalogues hundreds of NP-complete problems by domain (graphs, scheduling, storage, games).
- Advice on coping with intractability — special cases, approximation, exponential algorithms — is part of the book, not an afterthought.
- Cook and Karp are the cited founding papers; later PCP/approximation theory is outside its 1979 scope.
- W. H. Freeman, San Francisco, 1979 (ISBN 0-7167-1044-7 / 0-7167-1045-5); x+338 pages.
