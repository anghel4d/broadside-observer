# Trefoil Probabilities and Polyhedra

## One-sentence takeaway

Join six uniformly random points on the unit sphere into a closed hexagon: it is knotted (necessarily a trefoil) with probability exactly 1/(16 pi^2).

## Why it's lovely

Why you might love this: it is a Jane Street puzzle with a real answer. The proof turns a knot question into polyhedral combinatorics: the convex hull of six random points is almost surely one of two octahedra; the combinatorially regular one admits exactly one trefoil Hamiltonian cycle, the irregular one admits none. Stereographic projection then turns "probability of the regular hull" into a Sylvester-type random-triangle problem in the plane, solved with Stokes' theorem and Blaschke–Petkantschin. Answer chain: regular octahedron with probability 15/(4 pi^2), trefoil with probability 1/(16 pi^2).

## Key ideas

- Random closed hexagon from six i.i.d. uniform points on the sphere; the only possible nontrivial knot is the trefoil.
- Hull types: combinatorially regular vs non-regular octahedron (almost surely one of the two).
- Regular type: exactly one unoriented trefoil Hamiltonian cycle on the straight-line complete graph; irregular type: none.
- Stereographic projection to a Sylvester-type problem for the planar beta-prime measure dx dy / (pi (1 + x^2 + y^2)^2).
- Stokes plus Blaschke–Petkantschin give the expected squared mu-content of a random triangle, hence 15/(4 pi^2) and 1/(16 pi^2).

## Caveats

Specific to hexagons with vertices on the sphere; other models (e.g. uniform random equilateral polygons) and longer polygons are not covered by the exact formula. Twelve-page preprint, recent.
