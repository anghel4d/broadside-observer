# On Tables of Random Numbers

## One-sentence takeaway

Kolmogorov asks what it means for a *finite printed table* to be random, and argues that frequency / ensemble accounts do not settle the randomness of an individual sequence.

## Why it matters here

Companion to the 1965 complexity paper: this is the finite-string, engineering-facing side of algorithmic randomness — the right vocabulary for seed tables, hash nonces, and "is this noise texture actually noise?"

## Key ideas

- Classical probability talks about ensembles; a concrete table of digits is a single object.
- Frequency-stability conditions (von Mises style) are necessary but not sufficient for a finite table to deserve the name "random".
- Complexity / incompressibility is already in view as the missing individual-sequence criterion.
- Original: *Sankhyā* Ser. A 25(4), 1963, pp. 369–376; TCS 207(2), 1998 reprint carries the DOI.
