# Frobenius functors and one-sided Hopf algebras in braided monoidal categories

## One-sentence takeaway

In braided monoidal categories, a bimonoid is one-sided Hopf with invertible antipode exactly when its free Hopf-module functor is Frobenius.

## Why it's lovely

Why you might love this: the classical Hopf↔Frobenius handshake (antipode exists iff free Hopf modules are Frobenius) lifts cleanly into any braided monoidal world with mild assumptions — one-sided Hopf monoids included. It is the kind of dictionary that makes quantum algebra feel like category theory rather than calculation.

## Key ideas

- Works in any braided monoidal category with mild exactness/dualizability hypotheses.
- Links existence of antipode-like maps on bimonoid-like objects to the Frobenius property of the free Hopf-module functor.
- Characterizes one-sided Hopf monoids with invertible antipode via that Frobenius condition.
- Extends a classical Hopf-algebra fact to the braided setting Broadside already tracks (1585, braided cards).

## Caveats

Depends on braided monoidal background and technical dualizability hypotheses; not a new knot or TQFT invariant by itself. Read beside quantum Hopf rigidity (1585) and braided shelf.
