# The Non-Orientable Topology of Condorcet's Paradox

## One-sentence takeaway

Model strict rankings of three alternatives as a nerve complex, add simplices for preference cycles, and glue the contradictory cycles together: the gluing needs an orientation-reversing twist, so Condorcet's paradox becomes a Klein bottle (cycles left unrealised) or a real projective plane (cycles realised), while valid intransitive cycles such as rock-paper-scissors give an annulus or a sphere, and Arrow's theorem can be restated as a statement about orientability.

## Why it's lovely

A 240-year-old voting puzzle turns out to have a shape, and the shape is the one surface every topology student draws badly. The twist is not decoration. A cycle can run through the alternatives in either direction, and once transitivity makes every contradictory cycle say the same thing, identifying them forces the orientation flip. Rock-paper-scissors and a deadlocked electorate look identical as directed graphs, and the paper tells them apart by whether the surface can be oriented. For Broadside, any agent fleet that votes, ranks or merges preferences (judges, rerankers, ensembles) sits on this geometry; it is a clean picture of why no aggregation rule escapes the cycle.

## Key ideas

- **Baryshnikov's model, extended.** Rankings over three alternatives are simplices of a nerve complex. The authors add simplices for preference cycles so a model can include or exclude them.
- **Valid vs contradictory cycles.** Intransitive cycles (games) stay distinct; under transitivity all contradictory cycles are equivalent and get glued to one object, with a twist because cycles can be read in two directions.
- **Four surfaces.** Valid and unrealised gives an annulus, valid and realised a sphere, contradictory and unrealised a Klein bottle, contradictory and realised RP², each unique up to homeomorphism.
- **Arrow as orientability.** Arrow's impossibility theorem is reformulated as a statement about the orientability of the surface the aggregation must land on.
- **Lineage.** Builds on Chichilnisky–Heal topological social choice and on D'Antoni and Livson–Prokopenko, who showed Arrow generalises Condorcet.

## Caveats

Only three alternatives and strict preferences; the higher-dimensional and indifference cases are not classified here. It is a re-expression of known impossibility results, not a new one, and gives no voting rule. I read the abstract, the introduction and Table 1 (version 6, 27 pages).
