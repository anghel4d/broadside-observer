# Counting number-conserving cellular automata with radius 1

## One-sentence takeaway

A paper that *is* a program: it enumerates 1-D number-conserving radius-1 cellular automata and prints the counts through 7 states.

## Why it matters here

Why you might love this: literate combinatorics in the Iverson sense — the text computes the thing it is about. Number conservation is the falling-sand invariant (mass does not teleport). For Anoptic/Noita-shaped particle CA, knowing how many radius-1 mass-conserving rules exist, and having a program that counts them, is both a census and a generator of legal kernels.

## Key ideas

- Restricts to one-dimensional CA, radius 1, number-conserving (sum of states conserved).
- The article is executable: it computes the census rather than only proving a formula.
- Reports counts for alphabets of size up to 7.
- Number conservation is the discrete analogue of a continuity equation — the same constraint sand, traffic, and lattice-gas rules live under.

## Caveats

- 1-D radius-1 only; falling-sand in Anoptic is 2-D (or 3-D) with neighbourhoods larger than 3.
- Short 8-page 2026 note; treat counts as to-be-reproduced, not gospel.
- Does not construct visually interesting rules — it counts the conserving ones.
