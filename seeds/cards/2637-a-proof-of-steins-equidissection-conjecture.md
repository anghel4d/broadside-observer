# A Proof of Stein's Equidissection Conjecture

## One-sentence takeaway

No balanced polygon, meaning one whose boundary edges in every direction cancel in opposite pairs, can be cut into an odd number of equal-area triangles. This settles Stein's 2000 conjecture in nine pages and gives one proof for the centrally symmetric and polyomino cases.

## Why it's lovely

Monsky's 1970 theorem, that a square cannot be cut into an odd number of triangles of equal area, is a classic surprise. The statement is elementary, yet the classic proof colours the plane with a 2-adic valuation on the reals and then applies Sperner's lemma. Results for regular polygons, centrally symmetric polygons and polyominoes followed one at a time, each with its own technique. Stein's conjecture named the property they all share. Krachun builds on Rudenko's reduction of dissections to flows on trivalent graphs, extends it from the rationals to any number field, and the whole family falls. A footnote adds that balanced polygons are exactly the polygons that are equidecomposable by translations with a square of the same area (Hadwiger–Glur). The paper also states that the proof is the author's own work and that AI helped only with figures and exposition, a useful contrast to cards 2636 and 2640. For us it is a parity invariant of triangulations, the sort of hidden obstruction that is worth knowing about before trying to mesh or partition a shape evenly.

## Key ideas

- **Statement.** An equidissection cuts a polygon into triangles of equal area; Stein conjectured that no balanced polygon has an odd one.
- **History.** Thomas (1968) did the square when all vertices have rational coordinates with odd denominators, Monsky (1970) the square, Kasimatis (1989) regular n-gons, Monsky (1990) centrally symmetric polygons, and Stein (1999) and Praton (2002) polyominoes. Rudenko proved the rational-coordinate case of Stein's conjecture (2014, revised 2025).
- **Reduction.** Following Rudenko, a dissection becomes a K²-valued flow on a trivalent graph, where K is the number field generated by the coordinates. A vertex quantity A_v is the area form of two outgoing flow values.
- **New obstacle.** Rudenko worked over Z_(2), whose residue field is F₂, where three nonzero elements cannot sum to zero. Over the localisation of O_K at a prime above 2 the residue field can be larger and that fact fails.
- **Fix.** A more general cancellation theorem, proved with the geometry of rank-two lattices over that local ring and a homological lifting argument.

## Caveats

I read the abstract and introduction of v1 (Sunday 15 Nov 2026), which is not refereed. The paper is short, but the cancellation theorem is new mathematics and needs expert checking. It only rules out odd equidissections; it does not describe which even counts occur.
