# The Mathematical Theory of Communication

## One-sentence takeaway

The Illinois book reprints Shannon's 1948 BSTJ memoir and adds Weaver's three-level essay that carried information theory to non-engineers.

## Why it matters here

When Broadside notes say "Shannon–Weaver," they mean this book form, not the journal paper; keep the two cards distinct so citations stay honest.

## Key ideas

- Part II is Shannon's technical text, essentially the 1948 memoir with minor revisions.
- Weaver's opening essay splits communication into technical (symbols), semantic (meaning), and effectiveness (influence) problems, and insists Shannon solved only the first.
- Weaver popularizes entropy, redundancy, and channel capacity without the full proof apparatus.
- The 1949 cloth edition is the original book; Illinois later reprints (including the 1963 paperback, ISBN 978-0-252-72548-7) keep the same pairing.
- Cite this card for the book and Weaver's framing; cite the BSTJ card for theorems and formulas.
