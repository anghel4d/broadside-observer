# Introduction to Automata Theory, Languages, and Computation

## One-sentence takeaway

The 1979 “Cinderella book” standardizes the undergraduate spine of automata, grammars, decidability, and complexity in one volume.

## Why it matters here

Broadside’s foundations pool treats NFA/DFA/CFG/TM material as shared vocabulary. This is the textbook those cards assume — constructions, pumping lemmas, and reductions in one place — not a substitute for Rabin–Scott or Chomsky.

## Key ideas

- Finite automata, regular expressions, and the Myhill–Nerode view are developed together.
- Context-free grammars, pushdown automata, and parsing sit in the middle third.
- Turing machines, undecidability, and an introduction to complexity close the book.
- Rabin–Scott, Chomsky’s three models, and Kleene’s nerve-net events are the cited primary sources.
- Addison-Wesley, 1979, ISBN 0-201-02988-X, 418 pages; later HMU editions differ.
