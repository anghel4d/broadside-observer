# Bistable Quad-Nets Composed of Four-Bar Linkages

## One-sentence takeaway

Bistable spatial four-bar assemblies are discrete-differential-geometry objects: Study-quadric quad nets built from infinitesimally flexible Euclidean nets via Whiteley de-averaging — no numerical optimization.

## Why it's lovely

Why you might love this: snap-through mechanisms grown from discrete minimal surfaces; DDG that you can hear click.

## Problem

Bistable linkage assemblies are usually optimized numerically. Is there a purely geometric construction that scales to unbounded link counts and controls snap angles?

## Design

- Interpret linkages as quad nets in the Study quadric.
- Start from infinitesimally flexible Euclidean quad nets; apply Whiteley de-averaging.
- Existence proof for assemblies with unbounded links/joints; axis positions and snap angles under geometric control.
- Discrete minimal surfaces as a seed class.

## Evidence

Existence theorems + constructive geometric recipe; situates bistability inside DDG rather than black-box optimizers.

## Limitations

Not a drop-in game physics solver; Study-quadric language is specialist. Compliant origami continuum (Zhang et al.) is a sibling, not reminted.

## Implications for Broadside

Curiosity geometry with a craft echo for Anoptic: programmable bistability as discrete geometry, kin to 1028/1369 rather than FEM soup.

## Bottom line

Mint the clickable DDG. Bistability as a theorem about quad nets.
