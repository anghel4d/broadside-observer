# Recursively enumerable sets of positive integers and their decision problems

## One-sentence takeaway

Post classifies r.e. sets (creative, simple, hypersimple) and asks whether there is an incomplete non-recursive r.e. degree—the question that launches degree theory.

## Why it matters here

Shows undecidability is not a boolean. When Broadside talks about "structure inside the unsolvable" or about reductions between agent-search problems, it is using the landscape this address opened.

## Key ideas

- Recursively enumerable sets are those generated by a finite process; their complements need not be.
- *Creative* sets (the halting problem is the prototype) are m-complete among r.e. sets; *simple* sets are r.e. with an immune complement, hence non-recursive but not complete in the obvious way.
- Post's problem: is there an r.e. set that is neither recursive nor complete? Left open here; solved by Friedberg and Muchnik in 1956–57.
- Founding paper of Turing-degree theory as a subject, not merely a definition.
