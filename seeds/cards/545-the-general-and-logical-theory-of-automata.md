# The General and Logical Theory of Automata

## One-sentence takeaway

Von Neumann's Hixon lecture compares natural organisms with artificial automata and argues that complexity, reliability, and self-reproduction are the next problems after the stored-program machine.

## Why it matters here

Bridge from EDVAC organs to self-reproducing automata and to reliable computation from unreliable parts. Anoptic's "engine as organism" talk—fault-tolerant buses, reconstructible state, simulation that copies its own description—starts in this 1948 Caltech lecture (printed 1951).

## Key ideas

- Delivered at the Hixon Symposium, Caltech, September 1948; printed in Lloyd Jeffress (ed.), *Cerebral Mechanisms in Behavior*, Wiley, 1951, pp. 1–41.
- Complexity thresholds: below a certain size an automaton can only decay; above it, it can construct more complex automata.
- Analogies and disanalogies with the nervous system (McCulloch–Pitts grain, digital vs analog, precision).
- Sets up the later *Automata Studies* multiplexing paper and the unfinished self-reproduction manuscript Burks would edit.
