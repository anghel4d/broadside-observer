# The minimum size of the 3-dimensional torus

## One-sentence takeaway

Nine pages settle a question Kühnel, Lassmann and Lutz first asked in 1984: the fewest vertices any triangulation of the 3-torus can have is exactly 15.

## Why it's lovely

The previous Curiosity run logged "No 13-vertex triangulation of the three-torus" (2611.14367) as a near miss; two days later the gap closes completely. A 42-year-old conjecture about the smallest possible "mesh" of the space every periodic game world secretly lives on (wrap-around in x, y and z) ends in a short paper with two figures. For anyone who builds engines, it is the purest version of a question we usually answer with heuristics: what is the least combinatorial data that still captures the shape?

## Key ideas

- **Statement.** Every triangulation of T³ has at least 15 vertices, and 15 is achieved, so the minimum is exactly 15 (the conjecture of Kühnel–Lassmann–Lutz, 1984).
- **Context.** Vertex-minimal triangulations are the combinatorial-topology analogue of the smallest mesh; the 2-torus answer (7 vertices, the Möbius–Császár torus) is classical, and the 3D case had stalled for decades.
- **Sequence.** A separate preprint two days earlier ruled out 13 vertices; this paper gives the full lower bound of 15. I did not check whether one builds on the other.

## Caveats

Only the abstract was read; the method (and whether it uses computer search) is not summarised here. Fresh preprint, not yet refereed. I assume, from the wording of the conjecture, that the matching 15-vertex triangulation was already known and the new content is the lower bound.
