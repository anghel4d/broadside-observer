# Directed Wada cascade in systems with discrete scale invariance

## One-sentence takeaway

In dissipative systems whose equations are unchanged by a dilation there is an infinite ladder of attractors, each an exact magnified copy of the last; the natural guess is that all their basins are Wada-entangled, but the paper shows the boundaries nest in one direction only, a structure the authors call a directed Wada cascade that standard Wada tests do not detect.

## Why it's lovely

A Wada basin is the famous picture where every boundary point touches three or more basins at once. Here the dynamics cannot tell zooming in from zooming out, so symmetry suggests maximal entanglement of the whole infinite ladder. The authors find that this fails, and fails precisely: dilation covariance plus a single trapping region bound which boundaries a point can lie on, the bound depends only on its distance to the origin, no point lies on the boundary of all basins, and a point lies only on the boundaries of the deeper basins. The ladder is built by log-periodic modulation of a homogeneous nonlinearity through mode locking. The bridge to Anoptic is the picture of a scale ladder whose rungs are exact copies: Radiance Cascades also chains levels that differ by a fixed dilation, and this is a reminder that a self-similar hierarchy can still have a built-in direction (deeper levels see the boundary, shallower ones do not). That is a metaphor, not a result about rendering.

## Key ideas

- **Setting.** Maps and flows whose nonlinearity is a log-periodic modulation of a homogeneous function, giving exact discrete scale invariance and a ladder of attractors and trapping regions.
- **Expected.** With no distinction between magnification and its inverse, expect the Wada property for the whole ladder.
- **Found.** Covariance plus one trapping region imply an exact bound, depending only on distance to the origin, on which basin boundaries a point can lie on; no point is on the boundary of all of them.
- **Direction.** Points lie on boundaries of the deeper basins; this is proved for the stable manifolds of the boundary saddles and supported numerically for the rest of the boundary. Standard Wada tests as usually applied do not detect it.
- **Generality.** Shown in several maps differing in invertibility, nonlinearity, scaling exponent and dimension, and in one flow.

## Caveats

I read the abstract, introduction and conclusions of v1 (11 Nov 2026, 14 pp.); I did not check the proofs or rerun the numerics. It is not refereed. The statement for the full boundary is numerical, not proved; the authors say the rest of the boundary is assumed. The link to Radiance Cascades is an analogy only.
