# Continuous Game of Life: cell emergence and self-organization at the edge of growth

## One-sentence takeaway

A minimal continuous-space, continuous-time Game of Life (SmoothLife lineage) whose cell-like patterns self-replicate, glide, and die — and that self-organises at a dilute-to-dense transition once a global resource conservation law is added.

## Why it matters here

Why you might love this: Conway, but as a field; SmoothLife, but with a biophysicist's bifurcation diagram. Jülicher is on it; the code is public. Cells emerge, glide, and proliferate from an isotropic rule, then a conservation law parks the system at the edge of growth. That is procedural life as an ECS field, and a reaction–diffusion reading of the survival rule as a morphogen cascade. Anoptic already wants particle CA; this is the continuum twin you can actually watch.

## Key ideas

- Minimal continuous-space/time variant of Rafler's SmoothLife; isotropic rules yield cell-like units that replicate, glide, and vanish.
- Phenomenology is tied to homogeneous-state bifurcations, symmetry breaking, shape instabilities, and a dilute-to-dense proliferation transition.
- Maps onto a large reaction–diffusion system: homeostatic morphogen concentrations generated by a cell-sourced cascade of auxiliary reactions.
- A global conservation law (limited resource) drives self-organisation *at* that transition — "edge of growth."
- Lives at the science/computer-art border on purpose.

## Caveats

- "To be published in Artificial Life"; confirm the journal version against this preprint.
