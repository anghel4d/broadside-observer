# Recursive predicates and quantifiers

## One-sentence takeaway

Kleene classifies number-theoretic predicates by the number of unbounded quantifier alternations over a recursive matrix—the arithmetical hierarchy \(\Sigma^0_n / \Pi^0_n\).

## Why it matters here

Ancestor of descriptive complexity and of "how hard is this property to define" thinking in Broadside. When GRID COMMAND asks whether a standing rule is a local filter, an r.e. search, or a true \(\Pi^0_2\) claim, it is using this ladder.

## Key ideas

- A predicate is \(\Sigma^0_{n+1}\) (resp. \(\Pi^0_{n+1}\)) if it is an existential (universal) quantification of a \(\Pi^0_n\) (\(\Sigma^0_n\)) predicate; \(\Sigma^0_0 = \Pi^0_0\) are the recursive predicates.
- Normal-form theorems reduce every arithmetical predicate to a bounded number of alternations in front of a primitive-recursive kernel.
- Recursively enumerable sets are exactly the \(\Sigma^0_1\) sets.
- Sets the stage for the 1955 hierarchy paper and for Post's r.e.-degree landscape.
