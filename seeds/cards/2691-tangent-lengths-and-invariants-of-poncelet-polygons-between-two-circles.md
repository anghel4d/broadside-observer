# Tangent lengths and invariants of Poncelet polygons between two circles

## One-sentence takeaway

A polygon inscribed in one circle and circumscribed about another slides around in a one-parameter family by Poncelet's porism, and this paper finds new quantities that stay constant along the motion, including that the sum of fourth powers of the distances from the circumcentre to the contact points plus twice the squared area is constant.

## Why it's lovely

Poncelet's porism says that if one polygon closes up between two circles, every starting point gives another closed polygon: the whole family rolls around like a linkage. The paper studies it through the tangent lengths from the vertices to the inner circle and gets a pile of elementary conserved quantities, an n-gon version of Euler's formula, and a second quadratic invariant beside Weill's theorem, many with elementary proofs and some with two (one without derivatives). Reciprocation at a limiting point of the two circles turns them into statements about billiards in ellipses, giving short proofs of theorems of Akopyan–Schwartz–Tabachnikov and Bialy–Tabachnikov. The elliptic curve of the family then shows that the area determines the polygon up to reflection, finds the polygons of largest and smallest area (proving two conjectures of Cieślak and Mozgawa), and finds the points whose product of distances to the vertices is constant. For Broadside the bridge is the habit of looking for invariants of a one-parameter motion, which is exactly what a conserved-quantity test for a physics integrator checks.

## Key ideas

- **Setup.** Poncelet polygons between two fixed circles form a one-parameter family; track tangent lengths from vertices to the inner circle.
- **Invariants.** Sums of powers of the circumcentre-to-contact distances combine with area into constants; an n-gon version of Euler's formula; a second quadratic invariant going with Weill's theorem; an extension of a Kvant problem on bicentric hexagons.
- **Billiards.** Reciprocation at a limiting point of the two circles converts the invariants to billiards in ellipses and shortens known theorems.
- **Elliptic curve.** The curve of the family shows area determines the polygon up to reflection, gives the extremal-area polygons (two Cieślak–Mozgawa conjectures), and the points of constant product of distances to the vertices.

## Caveats

I read the abstract and skimmed the introduction of v1 (16 Nov 2026, 35 pp., 2 figures); I did not check the proofs. It is not refereed, and the author says comments are welcome. The acknowledgments say Claude and GPT were used for help with English. There is no engineering result here; it is pure Euclidean geometry.
