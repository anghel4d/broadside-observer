# Ice drives the emergence of functional protocells

## One-sentence takeaway

Slowly freezing and thawing a solution that is too dilute to do anything squeezes solutes and lipids together in the liquid veins between ice crystals and then lets them close up into cell-sized vesicles, so a reconstituted transcription–translation system of about 70 molecules starts making protein inside the vesicles although the bulk mixture shows little or no activity.

## Why it's lovely

The hard part of the origin of life is not making the molecules but getting enough of them into the same small place at once; diffusion is the enemy. Ice is a free, indiscriminate concentrator, since a growing crystal pushes out almost everything that is not water. This paper shows the whole chain in one experiment: concentration in the ice, compartment formation on the slow thaw, and a working multi-part biochemical machine switching on inside. It is a physical route from "chemistry" to "a cell-like thing that does something" that needs no machinery at all, and it applies as well to icy moons as to early Earth. There is also a systems lesson in it: a process that only works above a threshold concentration can be rescued by a cycle that gathers its parts rather than by a better catalyst.

## Key ideas

- **Slow steps matter.** Of nine freeze/thaw speed combinations, only those with a slow step (0.5 K/min cooling from 4 °C to −27.5 °C, or the same warming) produced micrometre-scale giant vesicles; fast freezing in liquid nitrogen scarcely made any.
- **Lipid chemistry matters.** DOPC (two unsaturated chains) beat PLPC and POPC. After slow/slow cycling DOPC vesicles held 4.60 ± 1.52% of input calcein against a 0.36% geometric expectation for evenly dispersed 100 nm vesicles, pointing to enrichment and fusion into larger compartments.
- **Co-concentration.** In situ imaging showed chemically diverse solutes and membrane material gathering together in ice-confined liquid regions during slow freezing.
- **Function.** The PURE in vitro transcription–translation system, diluted until it could no longer synthesize protein and mixed with small lipid particles, produced protein inside giant vesicles after freeze–thaw.

## Caveats

PURE is a modern, purified enzyme system, so this shows the physics can assemble a functional compartment, not that prebiotic chemistry had such parts to assemble. Phospholipids like DOPC are also modern; prebiotic membranes were probably fatty acids. Yields are per-vesicle and heterogeneous. Preprint, not yet peer reviewed.
