# Syntactic Structures

## One-sentence takeaway

Chomsky's 1957 monograph launches generative grammar: a language is the set of sentences a finite rule system generates, not a corpus of habits.

## Why it matters here

Formal language theory, compiler front ends, and cognitive claims about "what a grammar is" all feed through this Mouton book.

## Key ideas

- Phrase-structure rules generate kernel sentences; optional transformations produce passives, questions, and related forms.
- Adequacy is measured against native-speaker judgments of grammaticality, not against Shannon-style letter prediction.
- Behaviorist accounts (notably Skinner) are rejected: syntax is a generative competence, not a stimulus–response chain.
- Original: Mouton, The Hague, 1957, Janua Linguarum Series Minor 4. De Gruyter DOI 10.1515/9783112316009 is the stable digital reprint.
- The 1956 IRE paper is the technical preview; the 1959 "Formal Properties" paper axiomatizes the hierarchy.
