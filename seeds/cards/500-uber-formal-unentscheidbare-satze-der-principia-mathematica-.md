# Über formal unentscheidbare Sätze der Principia Mathematica und verwandter Systeme I

## One-sentence takeaway

Any consistent formal system strong enough for arithmetic harbors a true but unprovable sentence, and (second theorem) cannot prove its own consistency.

## Why it matters here

The hard stop on Hilbert's program; every Anoptic discussion of total verification or self-certifying systems sits downstream of arithmetized syntax.

## Key ideas

- Gödel numbering: proofs, formulas, and substitution become primitive-recursive predicates on integers.
- First incompleteness: the sentence that says of itself "I am not provable" is true if the system is consistent, hence unprovable in it.
- Second incompleteness: a consistency statement \(\mathrm{Con}(T)\) is likewise unprovable in \(T\), so finitary self-consistency proofs of Principia-like systems fail.
- Explicitly targets *Principia Mathematica* and related systems; published *Monatshefte* 38:173–198, DOI 10.1007/BF01700692.
