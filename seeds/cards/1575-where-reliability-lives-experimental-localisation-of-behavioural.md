# Where Reliability Lives: Experimental Localisation of Behavioural Properties in an Agent System

## One-sentence takeaway

Reliability properties of agentic systems have a location — model or machinery — and that location is an experimental question: a settlement ledger with separated mind/institution/world lets you intervene on each side and measure where the property actually lives.

## Why it matters here

Broadside's model-vs-harness failure localization finally has an experimental geometry, not a slogan. Provenance, belief availability, and enforcement sit in the institution; swapping or ablating cognition is a controlled axis — exactly how we should audit GRID COMMAND agents.

## Key ideas

- Persistent simulated settlement; append-only ledger is the only reality; every act is adjudicated against world state.
- Mind, institution, and world separated before any experiment — location of each reliability claim is testable.
- Institution interventions (evidence provenance, belief availability, physical-evidence legibility) and cognition interventions (ablate native minds, mid-task reset, frozen frontier-LLM panel, belief corruption).
- Preregistered experiments twice refuted the authors' central prediction in opposite directions; a staged veridical witness flipped marginal value of the belief channel from non-positive to positive (9/11 seeds).

## Caveats

- Custom settlement sim, not a production coding or RTS harness — transfer of the method, not the numbers.
- Heavy experimental protocol; expensive to replicate as a Broadside eval fixture without a thinner cut.
- Complements Outcome Monitors (1144) and Agent Zero Memory (1541); does not replace them.
